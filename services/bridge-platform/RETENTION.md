# Retention implementation status

The owner has approved the proposed refund windows and retention periods as business decisions. The complete legal documents and their operational implementation remain unfinished. This module neither approves the full legal text nor supplies the missing legal operator, contact address, jurisdiction, processors, transfer arrangements or rights-request process. It does not run automatically or expose an HTTP endpoint.

`retention.cjs` provides an isolated, dry-run-first primitive for a protected operator job. `planRetention(state)` does not mutate its input. `summarizePlan(plan)` omits member IDs and record keys and is the appropriate value for ordinary logs; the full plan is protected operational data. `policyFromDecisions` reads the approved business windows in `app/legal-content.cjs` into an explicitly draft engine policy: ninety days for timestamped IP associations and ended bans, thirty days for confirmed Bridge-profile cleanup, thirteen calendar months for recovery history, and annual permanent-ban review. Approval of those windows does not approve executable cleanup configuration or the complete legal documents. The default policy blocks execution. A protected runner must supply the matching reviewed policy and approval hash. An older reviewed recovery-only policy does not silently enable the new cleanup categories.

After an actual policy review, a protected runner can provide the approved policy and matching review hash to `applyRetentionPlan`. The plan binds the full source digest, revision, policy and holds, has a ten-minute execution window, and must be regenerated if any changes. Application returns a clone; the caller must persist it using one guarded store save with the original expected revision. A conflict requires a fresh plan and review. Never read approval or hold configuration from a desktop request or expose this primitive as a public API.

The current scope can remove expired enrollment/legal/replay nonces, expired work leases, recovery events older than thirteen calendar months, eligible timestamped IP associations and ended-ban history, and an allowlist of residual local profile fields after confirmed Bridge-only deletion. It keeps active/boundary leases and the full rolling-year recovery history. Normal license recovery now counts the rolling year without deleting older recovery evidence; only the reviewed planner removes old history. Request replay markers remain at exact expiry until the accepted clock-skew window has passed. An expired work lease is already invalid for execution; deleting it does not refund a reservation or reset weekly quotas. Unknown/malformed records are retained and reported. Invalid audit chains stop execution. A compact audit entry is appended only when something changed.

New authenticated IP observations store only an HMAC and `{version:1,firstObservedMs,lastSeenMs}` under `member.ipAssociations`; `ipHashes` remains the compatible search/ban index. A legacy string receives a timestamp only when that exact IP is actually observed again. No association date is inferred from an account's `lastSeenMs`, and unrelated legacy associations remain undated and retained. Timestamped associations are eligible only when strictly older than the configured period and no active or recent ended ban protects the subject or shared IP. Raw IP values are never added by this metadata code.

Ordinary platform transactions archive already expired bans into `banHistory`. Unban and replacement preserve the old record with `archiveVersion`, actual `endedAtMs`, `archiveRecordedAtMs`, and an end reason. This does not activate or extend an expired ban. A new permanent ban records its initial review and next annual review date. The protected full plan lists due or missing permanent-ban reviews; summary logs report only their count. A review deadline never automatically lifts a permanent ban. Ended-ban records must have the expected scope and trustworthy end metadata before ninety-day expiry, and they remain protected by relevant holds, including a held account sharing an associated IP.

Confirmed Bridge-only deletion records an allowlisted `member.deletion` receipt: exact canonical subject, Bridge client/app number, fresh platform operation ID, completed confirmation, membership generation, membership removal, session revocation, observation time and an explicit `globalAccountDeleted:false`. New authority replies must bind the operation's exact request ID and completion; legacy-shaped replies are refused for new operations. Rejoin rotates the membership generation, and a delayed deletion cannot commit against the new generation. Previously stored legacy confirmed-deletion metadata remains readable for retention; accepting it from storage does not authorize replaying it as a new authority response. This records the trusted adapter's confirmation; it does not implement or verify the still-unknown primary account routes. Interactive Bridge deletion clears the existing local profile/sign-in fields and retains security associations for their appropriate review/expiry. Later cleanup can clear only residual `username`, `email`, `devices` and `legal` fields after thirty days. It never deletes the member tombstone, balances, credit lots/debt, grants/licenses, subscriptions, orders, provider mappings, usage/quota or recovery history. A deleted flag, timestamp, wrong-subject receipt or global-deletion result alone is insufficient. Reactivation or any other source change invalidates a previously reviewed plan.

Subject holds preserve that subject's records; unidentifiable records are also retained when any explicit or financial subject hold is active. A global hold retains every candidate. Open or unknown provider dispute states and pending fraud-warning review records conservatively hold their bound subjects, including unidentified expired nonces and work leases that cannot safely be excluded from that hold; an unresolved financial binding holds every candidate until it is repaired. `actionable:false` is only an observed provider snapshot and does not resolve a pending manual review. Shared-IP ban history is preserved for a held member even when the ban targeted another account. Active work leases also prevent residual deleted-profile cleanup.

This does **not** complete the proposed retention policy:

- Untimestamped legacy IP associations, malformed dates and incomplete ban history require operator review. No migration invents missing dates, and the new tests do not prove that old production data is ready for cleanup.
- Permanent-ban review and fraud-warning resolution need an actual protected operator workflow. The owner warning page is read-only. Pending review records are not automatically acknowledged, refunded, closed or purged.
- Global account deletion remains dependent on an independently verified primary authority. Internal completion receipts are a fail-closed adapter contract, not evidence that its actual routes have been integrated. Global records and unconfirmed deletion flags are never eligible for this Bridge-only profile cleanup.
- The audit log is a hash chain. Filtering old entries would break verification. A verified archive/checkpoint, financial-event separation and legal-hold review must precede twelve-month audit deletion.
- The service retains only the current weekly usage aggregate; no eight-week detailed-usage archive exists here. Expiring leases is not proof that all process, proxy or security logs have been purged.
- Raw security logs, support tickets and backup copies live outside this scoped state. Their inventories, expiry jobs, protected deletion journal, restore-before-serving reapplication and actual storage lifecycle need separate verification. The production binary/configuration recovery archive is not an account-database backup and this module must not delete it.
- Orders, payments, provider mappings, credit funding lots/balances/debt, licenses, subscriptions, disputes, financial audit and account-wide entitlement history are untouched. Their minimal financial record categories and approved retention periods require a separate reviewed implementation. A generic age filter must not destroy payment idempotency or permit replayed purchases/refunds.

Do not claim that the thirty-day account/backup targets, ninety-day IP limits, twelve-month audit/support periods, or seven-year financial proposal are live based on these isolated tests. There is no automatic scheduler or retention HTTP endpoint. No production state, account or backup has been changed.
